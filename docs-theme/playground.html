Try a placeholder

Give it some variables and a placeholder, and it shows what comes out: at each step of the chain, and whether that is text, a number, true or false, or a list. It runs the card's own code, so what you see here is what a card gets. Nothing leaves your browser.

Whether a value is text or a number matters when the placeholder is the whole value of an option - columns: '[[count]]' gets the number 3. Inside a longer piece of text everything becomes text. Both are shown below.

To build a whole card from your templates - the final YAML, the warnings and the debug report - use Try a template. To make a template from cards you already have, use the template builder.

Your own entities

Add entities to the sample house below, one entry each. domain: is all an entry needs; count: 20 makes twenty, numbered 01 to 20 wherever [[index]] is written. Areas, floors and labels are found by id or name, and made when there is none. Anything not given is left out.

Item and function templates

Templates with an item: body, as under decluttering_templates:, for [[name|with:...]] and [[name|repeat:list]] to fill. Their default:, let: and extends: work as on a dashboard. A template with a function: instead is a step of its own, used as [[room|name]].

Things to try

The sample house

A web page has no Home Assistant, so the steps that ask one - area, state, friendly_name, last_changed and the rest - read this made-up house instead: lights, temperature and humidity sensors, doors and motion, in rooms on two floors, with a few labels, so a repeat: over a domain:, area:, floor: or label: has something to find. Use these entity ids, as a variable or written straight in the placeholder. Entities you add above are listed here too. To try them against your own Home Assistant, paste the sandbox dashboard into a new dashboard.

Every one of them can have its state read here. On a real card, |state only answers for entities the card lists in rebuild_on. And translate needs your Home Assistant's language, so here it gives back what it was given.

Sharing an example

The address of this page follows what is in the boxes, so Copy link - or the address bar - gives a link that opens the page with the same example in it. It is all in the part after #, which your browser never sends anywhere.

A link can also be written by hand, for a page that wants a "try it" link: playground.html#placeholder=...&variables=..., with entities=, templates= and only=1 as well if they are wanted, each URL-encoded. playground.html#placeholder=%5B%5Broom%7Cupper%5D%5D&variables=room%3A%20Hall opens with [[room|upper]] and room: Hall.
